# ADR 0005 — Experimental arms and the bounded correction round

Date: October 16, 2026. Status: implemented and tested offline with scripted models; no paid provider has been called. Covers P4.4. Spending caps (P4.5) and lease expiry, admission fencing, and retries (P4.6) remain open. See [implementation status](IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS.md).

**Arms are a fixed table over existing profiles.** `assay.contracts.policy.ARMS` maps A to `baseline`, B to `checked`, C to `reviewed` with the ordinary summary view, D to `checked_reviewed` with the ordinary view, and E to `checked_reviewed` with the evidence view. `run_arm_episode` builds the arm's review configuration and refuses any reviewer or summarizer the arm does not use: A and B take neither, C and D need both, and E needs a reviewer but no summarizer, because the host builds its view. `run_analyst_episode` also refuses an arm whose profile or reviewer view disagrees with the table. Every arm uses the same analyst prompt, tools, claim schema, limits, host integrity checks, and publisher; a test asserts that every arm sends the analyst an identical first request. Episode records carry the arm, and their validator rejects an arm/profile/view mismatch.

**One correction round, whose feedback is the arm's own decision.** When a revision is decided `rejected` or `needs_review`, the runner may offer one correction round (`EpisodeLimits.max_corrections`, 0 or 1, default 1). The analyst receives a fixed instruction plus a `correction-feedback-1.0` record: the round, the rejected revision ID, the outcome, the publisher's decision reasons, and the reviewer's reason codes. The reasons come from the arm's own policy decision, so A and C never see research-check outcomes even though the checks ran; reviewer prose is never included. Approved and stale revisions get no correction. The corrected submission becomes a new revision of the same report under the same attempt, and it is checked, reviewed, decided, and published like the first. Both revisions, their checks, reviews, and decisions stay in the evidence store, and the episode record lists every revision and review ID.

**The correction spends the same budgets.** The round shares the episode's tool-call, model-call, and wall-time limits; nothing is reset. A second review is new oversight usage, and episode oversight totals sum every review, including summaries. A combined oversight cap that is enforced before dispatch is P4.5. If the round ends without a decided revision (no submission, a limit, or a model or infrastructure error), the first revision's withheld decision remains the outcome. `correction_status` records why the round ended, and `error_category` records a correction-round error.

**Transcripts stay valid for providers.** If the analyst submits partway through a turn with several tool calls, each remaining call gets a fixed `not_run` tool reply, so a correction-round request never contains unanswered tool calls. The analyst prompt is versioned `analyst-prompt-1.1`; its hash covers the system prompt and the correction instruction.
