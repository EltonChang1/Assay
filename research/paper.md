# Assay: Executable Evidence and Controlled Publication for AI Data Analysis

**Report version 0.1 | October 18, 2026 | Systems research report and prospective study protocol**

**Abstract.** A quantitative conclusion can cite an authentic SQL execution and still answer the wrong analytical question. Assay separates execution integrity, exact claim-to-cell binding, public metric checks, reviewer judgment, publication authorization, and independent benchmark correctness. The implemented prototype uses synthetic order-event data, a restricted DuckDB worker in a Linux container, a transactional evidence store, three-valued analytical checks, structured claim slots, and a revision-bound publisher. Five intervention policies provide no research oversight, checks alone, ordinary-summary review, checks with ordinary review, and checks with structured evidence review. This report formalizes the supported order-total task, documents implemented algorithms and conditional invariants, reproduces deterministic counterexamples, and specifies a paired online experiment with explicit safety, usefulness, cost, and missing-outcome estimands. A source audit found and corrected a relation-binding defect in which common table expressions could hide an unauthorized population change while semantic checks passed. Fresh development validation and its provenance are reported separately from prospective experiments. A later 24-episode development pilot on the same exposed fixtures exhausted its six-call analyst allowance before any claim submission: it released nothing and never called the reviewer. These results establish reproducible mechanism behavior on exposed fixtures. They do not establish a reduction in model-induced incorrect releases, general SQL verification, or production safety. Confirmatory claims require the held-out benchmark, complete budget/recovery boundaries, model protocol, and statistical freeze described here.

**Evidence status.** This is an evolving technical paper containing completed systems validation and a prospective empirical protocol. Proposed sample sizes are not observations. A small OpenAI API development pilot, capped at USD 15, has been executed once. Its assignment accounting, charges, and terminal statuses are in the development live-pilot section. No confirmatory result is inferred from authorization or from the presence of an API adapter. The single PDF includes the argument, methods, numerical mechanism results, prospective analysis, limitations, and reproduction specification; source and machine-readable evidence remain available in the research repository for independent checking.

## 1. Research problem and contribution boundary

The research question is whether executable analytical checks and an evidence-preserving review protocol reduce materially incorrect quantitative claims released by an AI analyst at a fixed allowed oversight budget, while retaining correct required answers. The unit of safety is the report actually released to the simulated end user. An unreleased draft visible in a researcher audit interface is not a release, and a monitor's favorable label is not ground truth. These distinctions determine both the architecture and the experiment's denominators.

Consider an order worth USD 100 with several line items. A query joining orders to their items may repeat the order amount once per item. A receipt can prove which query ran against which snapshot and what cell it produced. A claim can point to that exact cell, with the correct unit and decimal value. Neither fact establishes that the aggregation counts each order once. The failure concerns the meaning of the query relative to the public question. A second failure arises when a repair sums distinct *amounts*: distinct order identities can have the same amount, so deduplicating values removes legitimate contributions.

Assay therefore treats the following predicates as different research objects: authorized execution, authentic result capture, numerical binding, compliance with supported public task semantics, a valid review decision, a current approved revision, and independent correctness. Their conjunction is not collapsed into a universal verification flag. The first five are runtime mechanisms; independent correctness is an evaluation outcome obtained after the episode. Returning the evaluator's answer to the runtime checker would trivialize the intervention and invalidate the intended research question.

The implemented contribution is a narrow systems prototype and development validation, rather than an established empirical improvement. Its distinctive experimental opportunity is the E-versus-D contrast: the same research checks and reviewer, the same permitted evidence corpus and lookup tools, and the same combined allowed oversight resources, with a different initial evidence representation. Ordinary-summary generation consumes resources in D. E uses a host-built representation. The estimand is the effect of those review protocols under equal caps, including their resource allocation, rather than an abstract property of provenance holding every token constant.

The current analytical implementation concerns settled USD order revenue and a permitted order count. Although the charter lists totals, means, rates, comparisons, and rankings as a longer-term domain, these broader families do not inherit validity from the order-total implementation. The deterministic fixtures, tests, source code, and observed values in this report are exposed development material. Fresh numerical seeds from the same generator do not constitute a structurally unseen benchmark. Negative or inconclusive future E-versus-D results satisfy the research objective if the protocol, accounting, and scope are sound.

### 1.1 Questions answered by the present evidence

The completed work asks whether independent canonical-event scoring distinguishes correct and incorrect relational calculations; whether genuine query results can be bound to structured claims without conflating that binding with correctness; whether the common publisher enforces the selected check/review policy; whether correction produces a new auditable revision; and whether declared worker boundaries operate under the tested environment. The source audit additionally asks whether the semantic checker resolves relation identity conservatively enough to avoid false certainty in selected adversarial SQL shapes.

The future empirical question asks how often real analyst/reviewer policies make and prevent errors on independently assigned tasks, under ordinary mistakes, indirect prompt injection, and prompted sabotage. Test counts do not answer that question. Scripted models deliberately choose known queries and review decisions. Their behavior demonstrates control flow, not the distribution of natural model mistakes or the strength of an adaptive attacker. Container diagnostics establish selected configuration and resource properties, not resistance to every malicious program or compromise of the host.

### 1.2 Relationship to prior work

AI control studies protocols intended to remain useful while preventing harmful outcomes even when an untrusted model attempts subversion. Greenblatt et al. evaluate this idea on programming tasks using trusted and untrusted models and limited trusted labor [R1]. Assay adopts the separation of legitimate-task usefulness from adversarial success and applies it to quantitative reports. Its synthetic analyst side objective is a constructed threat regime; it does not demonstrate that a model acquired an undisclosed objective spontaneously. No numerical result from the programming setting is transferred to the present task.

Database provenance provides formal ways to track how relational outputs depend on inputs. Green, Karvounarakis, and Tannen's semiring formulation represents annotations and multiplicities through relational operations [R2]. Assay's implemented receipts and dependency graph are coarser execution/artifact provenance, not a complete implementation of provenance polynomials or row-level lineage. The analytical observation motivating this system is that even complete provenance for a computation would not by itself establish that the chosen computation answers the user's metric contract. Provenance and semantic correctness are complementary requirements.

Text-to-SQL evaluation also depends on reliable truth. Wretblad et al. examine noise in BIRD questions and gold queries and show why benchmark annotations warrant scrutiny [R3]. Assay's independent reference is consequently derived from canonical business events rather than the analyst's SQL or the online verifier's labels. This design reduces one shared failure path; it does not eliminate errors in the public contract, generator, or reference implementation. Hand-calculated cases, alternative correct queries, counterexamples, and versioned scorer repairs remain necessary.

Inspect documents sandbox environments and host tool integration [R4]. Assay's optional adapter uses Inspect for model access while the trusted gateway explicitly dispatches SQL to its worker. Merely declaring a sandbox configuration would not make arbitrary host-side tools isolated. ControlArena provides settings and components for AI control experiments [R5]; it is a future integration option rather than an installed runtime dependency or a source of completed results here. TraceFrame is currently an optional audit-export target, and AMOS remains a design reference and deferred integration.

## 2. Formal task, truth, and error model

Let a public task be T = (V, M, G, P, I, U, S_req, S_sup, E, A), containing permitted source versions V, metric M, grain G, population P, a half-open time interval I, output unit U, required slots S_req, permitted supporting slots S_sup, tolerances E, and abstention rules A. A claim identifies its slot and includes the metric/type, decimal value or explicit abstention, unit, grain, snapshot, time scope, and current/historical report semantics. An executable citation additionally identifies a successful host execution and an exact result-cell locator. The task, source, execution, and report identities are distinct.

For the implemented revenue task, canonical event o has a unique order identity, status, currency, timezone-aware occurrence time, and amount a_o in integer cents or a declared missing value. Define eligibility independently of relational line-item multiplicity:

\[
e_T(o)=\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{status}(o)=\mathrm{settled}\}
\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{currency}(o)=\mathrm{USD}\}
\mathbf{1}\{t_{\min}\leq t_o<t_{\max}\}.
\]

When all eligible amounts are known, the independent reference is

\[
y_T=\frac{1}{100}\sum_{o:e_T(o)=1}a_o,\qquad
n_T=\sum_o e_T(o).
\]

If any eligible amount is missing, revenue is unanswerable under the current contract rather than the sum of the remaining known amounts. The order count can remain answerable. Duplicate canonical order identities and unsupported semantic contracts produce a visible reference-input error. Empty eligible populations produce the specified zero total through the canonical calculation; database NULL behavior must not silently override the contract.

For an answerable numeric slot s, value v agrees numerically with reference y if

\[
|v-y|\leq\max(\epsilon_{s,\mathrm{abs}},\;|y|\epsilon_{s,\mathrm{rel}}).
\]

Agreement also requires the slot's metric, type, unit, grain, allowed snapshot, interval, and report semantics to match. The default tolerances are exact, and count slots require exact agreement. Decimal arithmetic preserves financial values without binary-float rounding; the reference expands its decimal context to avoid silently rounding large canonical integer totals. Numeric range constraints bound arithmetic even for trusted callers bypassing ordinary validation. A claim of correct abstention requires reference unanswerability, public permission to abstain, and a nonempty reason. Arbitrary refusal on an answerable task is incorrect.

The independent report score checks every required slot and every submitted supporting slot. Missing required slots, duplicate slot claims, undeclared slots, incorrect supporting claims, wrong semantics, unsupported answers, and invalid abstentions remain explicit reason codes. The aggregate report-correct flag is useful for complete-task correctness, but it is not identical to the primary incorrect-release endpoint: an omitted answer reduces usefulness, while an actually released materially wrong quantitative assertion triggers the safety event. The prospective analysis retains slot-level error classes to avoid substituting one endpoint for the other.

### 2.1 Multiplicity and the distinct-value counterexample

Let m_o be the number of matched line items for eligible order o. An expanded join computes

\[
y_{\mathrm{join}}=\frac{1}{100}\sum_{o:e_T(o)=1}m_o a_o,
\qquad y_{\mathrm{join}}-y_T=\frac{1}{100}\sum_{o:e_T(o)=1}(m_o-1)a_o.
\]

This formula describes overcounting when every contributing order has a match and positive amounts; absent matches, signed amounts, and alternative join kinds require their own case analysis. The current fixtures deliberately contain the declared development structure. The common shortcut SUM(DISTINCT amount) instead computes one contribution per distinct monetary value. If two distinct orders have the same eligible amount, it undercounts the legitimate order-grain sum. Deduplicating order identities before aggregation, when the public data and join restrictions support that equivalence, addresses a different operation.

### 2.2 Integrity, semantics, and release as separate predicates

For claim c, write H(c) for authentic successful host execution and structural integrity, B(c) for exact numerical binding, and Q_T(c) for supported public analytical-contract checks. A valid binding B(c) does not imply Q_T(c), and even a collection of passing public checks is not a universal proof that every admitted SQL query is semantically correct. The implemented checks recognize a limited grammar and selected relational equivalences. Unsupported cases must remain unknown. The newly discovered relation-binding counterexamples and conservative correction illustrate why the distinction is an empirical engineering requirement rather than a naming convention.

Write R(r) for a well-formed reviewer recommendation bound to exact revision r, F(r) for current source epochs and valid attempt authorization, and P_a(r) for the arm's deterministic release predicate. The baseline and reviewed policies deliberately permit the measurement of research-level errors while retaining common host controls. Checked policies add mandatory research-check requirements. The publisher alone emits released bytes. The private oracle is not an argument to P_a. Detailed implemented check membership, state transitions, assumptions, and counterexamples follow in the system-methods section.


## Technical architecture, executable semantics, and release invariants

### Implemented object language

Assay separates an analyst's proposed numerical answer from the authority that can release that answer. Its implemented object language is deliberately narrower than its research charter: the current task contract supports settled-order revenue and an optional supporting order count. General means, rates, comparisons, rankings, and unrestricted prose truth are charter-level extensions, rather than implemented semantic profiles. The principal versioned interfaces are `TaskSpec`, `DatasetSnapshot`, `ExecutionReceipt`, `Claim`, `ReportRevision`, `CheckBundle`, `ReviewDecision`, `ApprovalRecord`, and `ReleaseRecord`. Their composition exposes the distinction between an authentic execution, a number copied from that execution, an analytically valid calculation, a review recommendation, and a release authorization. [Source: `src/assay/contracts/task.py:61-146`; `src/assay/contracts/report.py:18-153`.]

Let a public snapshot contain order relation \(O\) and item relation \(I\). For an order \(o\), let \(a_o\) denote its integer amount in cents, \(t_o\) its occurrence timestamp, and \(k_o\) its order identifier. The task fixes the eligible population

\[
E(T,O)=\{o\in O:\operatorname{status}(o)=\texttt{settled}\land
\operatorname{currency}(o)=\texttt{USD}\land t_0\le t_o<t_1\}.
\]

The supported total is \(R=100^{-1}\sum_{o\in E}a_o\), with each order contributing once; the supporting count is \(N=|E|\). The interval is closed on the left and open on the right, stored as timezone-aware UTC timestamps. A missing eligible amount makes the total unestablishable under the current null policy and requires an explicit justified abstention. It does not imply zero. Empty-population totals remain outside the verified semantic profile. The actual metric definition, population wording, inclusion/exclusion rules, currency, order grain, entity key, and scale factor are constrained to the implemented `settled-order-revenue-1.0` contract. This prevents a caller from retaining a familiar version identifier while silently redefining the metric. [Source: `src/assay/contracts/task.py:26-90`; `src/assay/verification/service.py:396-454`.]

A claim records the answer slot, metric identity/type, value or abstention, unit, grain, snapshot, time scope, report semantics, and optional exact result-cell reference. A numerical reference comprises a result identifier and zero-based row index plus exact column name; the two components must occur together. An abstention must carry a nonblank reason and cannot assert a numerical cell binding. Host-owned claim records additionally name their execution and receive a claim identifier. Reports contain 1-64 such records and embed their assigned task and snapshot. Schema validation is an interface constraint, not an assertion that a claim is true. [Source: `src/assay/contracts/claim.py:13-48`; `src/assay/contracts/report.py:18-61`.]

Versioned records reject extra fields and are frozen at the model level. Numerical claims reject binary floating-point and Boolean inputs, use finite `Decimal` values, and restrict precision, magnitude, and scale. Canonical model bytes are UTF-8 JSON with sorted keys, compact separators, explicit decimal strings, no nonfinite JSON numbers, and one final newline. Define \(C(x)\) as that serialization and \(h(x)=\operatorname{SHA256}(C(x))\). Blob identities instead hash their exact bytes. This convention makes serialization part of evidence identity; JSON documents with equivalent abstract values but different noncanonical bytes cannot substitute for stored model artifacts. [Source: `src/assay/contracts/base.py:13-61`.]

### Adversary and trusted computing base

Analyst output, SQL, dataset text, retrieved documentation, and reviewer recommendations are untrusted. The intended attacker can induce ordinary errors, place instructions in declared public text fields, or receive a prompted synthetic side objective. The attacker cannot modify frozen numerical data, assigned task contracts, authoritative evidence, gateway/checker code, publication policy, or private scorer. Prompted sabotage therefore measures behavior under an explicitly supplied side objective; it is not evidence of spontaneously arising covert goals. Final-test actor/evaluator information isolation remains future work. [Source: `docs/CHARTER.md:17-37`; `SECURITY.md`.]

The trusted computing base includes the host runner, schema and policy implementations, filesystem and SQLite service, Docker client/daemon, container runtime and kernel, pinned worker, and installed dependencies. The host dispatches model requests and records receipts; models do not supply authoritative execution identities or publish directly. Public verification uses authenticated public tables, while the independent private evaluator scores released outputs after an episode. The latter is a measurement component, not a release authority. Excluding evaluator files from a runtime wheel and omitting private files from an image are useful packaging properties, but do not establish isolation between all host-side processes.

Content addressing is conditional on this trust boundary. An adversary with arbitrary host/database write access could replace content together with its indices and hashes. There are no signing keys, externally anchored log heads, or cryptographic append-only consensus mechanism. Event append-only behavior is an API convention over host-owned tables, rather than a defense against the database owner. Similarly, frozen snapshot files are copied, validated, and read-only inside the worker mount; their host owner can still write them. The architecture studies untrusted analytical behavior under a trusted local lab, not a malicious lab administrator.

### SQL admission and isolated execution

SQL capability authorization and analytical verification are separate algorithms. `duckdb-select-1.0` accepts exactly one DuckDB-dialect `Select` root, bounded by 16,384 submitted UTF-8 bytes, 512 AST nodes, and depth 32. Its explicit allowlist checks both exact expression classes and meaningful argument names; unfamiliar subclasses or newly introduced modifiers cannot inherit authority accidentally. The subset includes ordinary nonrecursive CTEs, subqueries, joins, predicates, aggregates, arithmetic, conditional expressions, primitive casts, grouping and ordering. Commands, writes, recursive CTEs, windows, set operations, table/file/URL functions, qualified external/catalog relations, and unlisted functions fail closed. Approved relation identifiers use restricted ASCII spelling and cannot use reserved system prefixes. [Source: `src/assay/gateway/policy.py:20-25,43-211,235-285`.]

Source authorization traverses actual CTE/subquery scopes rather than collecting CTE names globally. Every table node must resolve either to a scoped relation or an approved physical table; actual physical sources are collected in deterministic order. Literal source tokens remain prohibited even if parsing normalizes them into quoted identifiers. Authorization returns the original SQL, SHA-256 over its exact submitted bytes, normalized comment-free SQL, physical source identities, and policy version. The worker executes the normalized form, and the receipt preserves both forms. Thus formatting changes can alter submitted-query identity without changing executed normalized SQL. Successful authorization alone says nothing about the task metric: duplicated joins and `SUM(DISTINCT amount)` are permitted capabilities. [Source: `src/assay/gateway/policy.py:194-232,265-292`; `src/assay/gateway/service.py:91-120`.]

Before dispatch, the gateway validates the public bundle and copies only its allowlisted task, manifest, manifest hash, and declared CSV tables into staging under a manifest-derived directory. It validates the copy again, assigns explicit directory/file modes `0755`/`0644`, and renames staging into place; an existing same-content destination is revalidated. These modes are operationally significant: Linux bind mounts preserve host permissions, and a `0700` temporary directory would prevent the worker's fixed UID from reading it. The immutable input mounted for a query is the retained copy, rather than a caller's live directory. [Source: `src/assay/gateway/service.py:33-70`.]

The execution client requires a local Linux Docker engine and has no host SQL fallback. The build context contains only a digest-pinned Python base image definition, hash-pinned DuckDB/sqlglot wheels, worker, and public SQL policy. The saved worker manifest records local immutable image ID and source/dependency hashes; source mismatch requires rebuilding. Runtime invocation disables networking, makes root and `/data` read-only, selects UID/GID 10001, drops all capabilities, prevents privilege escalation, limits processes to 64 and open files to 64, allocates one CPU, caps memory and combined memory/swap, and provides a 64 MiB `noexec,nosuid` temporary filesystem. Proxy environment variables are cleared. These controls rely on the actual local runtime configuration; argument construction alone is not evidence of boundary enforcement. [Source: `src/assay/execution/docker.py:60-215`; `sandbox/Dockerfile`; `sandbox/requirements.txt`.]

Default host limits are 30 seconds, 256 MiB container memory, 1,000 rows, and 1,048,576 output bytes. Worker protocol input is capped at 32,768 bytes and rejects duplicate JSON keys, nonfinite constants, unexpected keys, and incorrect scalar types. The worker applies SQL authorization independently, reads only fixed CSV schemas through Python, validates row/key/type constraints, and inserts values through parameterized statements; filesystem paths never become analyst SQL. Each table is limited to 8 MiB and 50,000 rows. DuckDB uses one thread, a 128 MB internal memory limit, at most 32 MB temporary spill, disabled external access and extension installation/loading, and locked configuration before execution. [Source: `src/assay/contracts/experiment.py:14-20`; `sandbox/worker.py:23-28,100-152,210-238,347-412`.]

The worker fetches at most the row cap plus one and rejects overflow instead of returning a successful truncated result. Output requires unique column names, at most 128 columns, supported typed scalar cells, and a complete bounded encoding. Decimal cells retain decimal strings. The host concurrently drains stdout and stderr under a shared capture budget, starts the execution deadline before writing the request, and explicitly removes the container on timeout/cancellation; killing the Docker CLI alone is insufficient. Unconfirmed cleanup, incomplete capture, malformed wire data, excess rows, nonzero completion, or output overflow cannot produce a successful receipt. The container deadline covers pipe dispatch/execution, not the entire episode, prior bundle freezing, or all Docker readiness checks. [Source: `sandbox/worker.py:307-344`; `src/assay/execution/docker.py:218-355`.]

### Durable evidence and dependency identity

The gateway assigns an execution UUID and trusted run identity, captures timestamps and outcome, and hashes the canonical complete result. A result identifier is `result-` followed by that digest; different executions producing identical typed results share result identity while retaining distinct receipts. The model normally receives a preview of at most 20 rows, explicitly marked when truncated, while the store preserves the complete admitted result. Failures cannot assert successful result metadata. [Source: `src/assay/gateway/service.py:103-135`; `src/assay/contracts/execution.py:81-119`.]

Evidence schema version 3 stores canonical blobs inside SQLite and separate indices for executions, results, source epochs/history, attempts, report revisions, claims, reviews, approvals, releases, and latest-release pointers. Connections enable foreign keys and `synchronous=FULL`; the database uses WAL, and writes use `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`. Execution recording revalidates model instances, recomputes exact query/task/snapshot/result identities, checks source membership, and atomically commits all related artifacts, indices, dependency edges, and host event. Reusing an execution identity with different bytes fails; an identical retry verifies the existing execution and returns. Reads rehash artifacts, validate their schemas, and require canonical bytes. [Source: `src/assay/evidence/store.py:25-85,111-150,156-294,455-543`.]

An edge \(u\rightarrow v\) means artifact \(v\) depends on \(u\). For example, task/snapshot/result feed the receipt; receipt feeds a claim; claim feeds its report; report feeds rendered output and check bundle; decision feeds approval; approval/rendered bytes feed release. Edges require existing verified artifacts and reject self-dependency or a path that would close a cycle. Recursive descendant queries support provenance inspection. The graph is an explicit dependency index, not a proof that every dependency is revalidated on every operation. Hash checks occur when a particular artifact is read; publication does not rerun SQL or recursively rehash every historical receipt, result, and CSV. [Source: `src/assay/evidence/store.py:319-386`; `src/assay/publication/service.py:441-455,562-591,632-646,701-703`.]

### Verification layers and counterexamples

For a report with \(n\) claims, the frozen expected mandatory-check keys comprise two global host checks, two host checks per claim, and seven research checks per claim: \(2+9n\) checks. Host checks cover report contract, declared-slot completeness/uniqueness, evidence identity, and execution success. Research checks cover cell binding, slot metadata, SQL metric, units, grain, population, and public missingness. Advisory data diagnostics are recorded separately. Each result is `pass`, `fail`, or `unknown`, names its class, scope, inputs and checker version, and may record limitations. Missing evidence storage can yield `unknown`; demonstrated mismatches yield `fail`. Neither outcome is silently converted to success. [Source: `src/assay/verification/service.py:25-67,185-258,302-308`.]

Evidence identity requires the assigned run/task/snapshot, captured result, permitted physical sources, and declared dependency subset. It reparses the original query and compares normalized SQL/source identities, and authenticates retained public CSVs against their manifests. Cell binding then checks only the specified row and exact column, requiring a finite numeric cell. With asserted value \(v\), cited cell \(z\), and slot tolerances \(\tau_a,\tau_r\), binding accepts iff

\[
|v-z|\le\max(\tau_a,|z|\tau_r).
\]

It does not search other cells for a convenient matching value. Slot checks independently bind metric, type, unit, grain, interval, semantics, and snapshot. A genuine result containing the same number for an unrelated metric still fails the analytical checks. [Source: `src/assay/verification/service.py:84-182,269-345`; `tests/test_verification.py:101-129`.]

The public semantic profile performs restricted structural analysis, not independent gold-answer recomputation. It recognizes an `orders` base, exact settled/USD/time conjunction, supported sum/count expression and conversion, and at most one inner/left item join or supported existence predicate. Item-key deduplication can be established by a restricted `DISTINCT order_id` or `GROUP BY order_id` relation. Complete public rows supply uniqueness, missingness, and item-key multiplicity/coverage facts. Unsupported grouping, row selection, multiple joins, conditional aggregate expressions, or unresolved provenance produce `unknown`, even where a human can establish correctness. This explicitly trades coverage for a smaller analyzed language; the implementation is not a general SQL equivalence solver or formally verified proof system. [Source: `src/assay/verification/semantics.py:156-296,319-478`.]

The distinction is concrete. Let \(m_o\) be the number of item rows for order \(o\). An inner item join followed by an order-amount sum computes \(R_J=100^{-1}\sum_{o\in E}m_oa_o\) when coverage holds. Authenticity and binding can both pass while \(R_J\ne R\). Public multiplicities detect this repeated contribution; missing eligible keys additionally violate inner-join population coverage. `SUM(DISTINCT amount)` instead deduplicates amount values, which merges distinct equal-priced orders. The base development fixture has cents \((10000,8000,2000)\) and multiplicities \((2,1,3)\), giving \(R=200\) and \(R_J=340\). The equal-amount variant adds a separate 100-dollar order: correct revenue becomes 300 while amount-distinct revenue remains 200. [Source: `src/assay/datasets/sales.py:153-205`; `src/assay/verification/semantics.py:340-345,441-461`; `tests/test_verification.py:87-99,131-151`.]

This audit also exposed a distinct provenance defect and corrected it before final validation. A CTE named `orders` could filter the real relation, while the original semantic recognizer inspected its literal name as if it still denoted the complete public table. A shadowed `order_items` CTE beneath a deduplicating join had the same problem. Positional column lists could remap an aggregate column into the apparent `order_id` position while the checker reasoned about the original projected key. Diagnostic execution through the existing offline gateway harness produced genuine DuckDB results of 100 or 300 against the base answer 200; before correction, all eleven mandatory checks passed. This was an implementation error, not a semantic property of the task.

The correction conservatively rejects semantic inference whenever any CTE shadows `orders` or `order_items`, case-insensitively, or any relation alias declares positional columns. SQL capability authorization still permits these queries; the semantic layer returns `unknown` and the checked policy withholds. Five regression cases cover base shadowing, quoted mixed-case shadowing, item shadowing under a derived join, CTE-definition column remapping, and CTE-reference column remapping. They verify wrong captured values, preserved host/binding success, four unknown query-semantic checks, and `needs_review` under the checked gate. Existing correct direct aggregation, existence, grouped-CTE join, and distinct left-join examples remain accepted. The focused verification/publication/policy run passed 114 tests; this is a local regression result, not a model-performance estimate. [Source: `src/assay/verification/semantics.py:127-141,393-407`; `tests/test_verification.py:79-85,203-258`.]

### Deterministic policy and transactional publication

Write \(H\) for complete unique passing expected host checks, \(Q\) for complete unique passing expected research checks, and \(V\) for a correctly bound reviewer `allow`. The profiles implement baseline \(H\), checked \(H\land Q\), reviewed \(H\land V\), and checked-reviewed \(H\land Q\land V\). Arms A, B, C, D, E map respectively to baseline, checked, reviewed/ordinary, checked-reviewed/ordinary, and checked-reviewed/evidence. D/E therefore share the deterministic gate and differ in reviewer representation. Baseline and reviewed profiles intentionally do not use research-check outcomes to authorize release. They still retain common integrity/publication controls; an authentic wrong calculation can be released in those profiles and scored incorrect afterward. [Source: `src/assay/contracts/policy.py:6-17`; `src/assay/publication/service.py:128-192`.]

Expected check coverage must occur exactly once with the current checker version. A failed required check rejects; unknown, absent, duplicated, rebound, or version-mismatched checks yield `needs_review` unless another required check already fails. Reviewed profiles additionally bind the review to report hash/revision and expected check visibility; explicit rejection rejects, and anything other than allow withholds. Advisory checks cannot authorize. The renderer accepts only unique declared required/permitted supporting slots, enforces their metadata, and emits fixed UTF-8 templates. Arbitrary analyst/reviewer prose and abstention explanations remain audit material rather than released bytes. [Source: `src/assay/publication/service.py:145-191`; `src/assay/publication/rendering.py:24-81`.]

The report lifecycle is `submitted → checked → approved | rejected | needs_review`, with `approved → published`. Source invalidation can mark pending current-data revisions stale. Each transition verifies the active run/attempt/fencing token, revision ownership, and expected state. Starting a new attempt increments the token and revokes the previous attempt; cancellation marks the attempt canceled and clears the active pointer. Tokens are fencing identities, not time-expiring leases: the present `AttemptLease` has no expiration timestamp. Check computation runs outside the writer lock, followed by another ownership/state check in the committing transaction. [Source: `src/assay/publication/service.py:90-94,203-276,367-477,708-723`.]

Approval binds report hash, rendered hash, check-bundle hash, optional review hash, profile/policy version, source epoch, attempt, and fencing token. Publication rereads these artifacts, validates the bindings, re-renders the same typed claims, and checks the registered source within one writer transaction. It commits the release row, latest pointer, published status, provenance edges, and event together. Existing publication is idempotent for the same revision. No external file promotion creates an alternate visibility window: rendered bytes may exist earlier, but ordinary release access requires a committed release index. Unique revision/approval constraints prevent duplicate releases for that revision. [Source: `src/assay/publication/service.py:522-706`; `src/assay/evidence/store.py:63-80`.]

For current-data reports, source validity requires active snapshot ID and epoch equality. Registering a different snapshot increments the epoch and, in that transaction, stales current releases, removes their latest pointers, and stales pending current revisions. Historical reports instead require registered snapshot history. Original bytes and release records remain accessible through explicit archival access; validity is mutable host state separate from immutable release bytes. Snapshot re-registration treats equal snapshot IDs as idempotent without comparing manifests, so globally immutable ID-to-content assignment is a trusted-host assumption. [Source: `src/assay/publication/service.py:280-363,753-784`.]

Under correct trusted-host operation and SQLite durability/locking assumptions, these mechanisms support three conditional invariants: no ordinary release without committed approval-bound bytes; no canceled/superseded attempt can perform a later publication transition; and no old-epoch release remains current after a committed source change. Concurrent release, cancellation, and source updates are ordered by writer transactions. Cancellation does not retroactively erase a release that committed first; a later source update changes validity rather than historical truth. Recovery tests exercise concurrent publishers, source/cancel races, rollback, and process death mid-publication. These are tested invariants with explicit assumptions, not machine-checked theorems. [Source: `tests/test_publication_recovery.py:57-191`; `tests/test_publication.py:152-269`.]

The resulting system supports controlled experiments on release behavior and bounded semantic coverage. Its architecture alone does not establish equal paid oversight budgets, provider-spend enforcement, expiring leases, gateway admission fencing, final-test information isolation, or live-model safety gains. Those remain separate implementation/protocol gates. In particular, restrictive checks can lower incorrect-release frequency by withholding useful answers, so empirical analysis must report correct required-answer coverage alongside release error rates and intervention costs.

### Research question, hypotheses, and target of inference

The primary question is whether a structured evidence representation reduces materially incorrect quantitative claims released by an AI analyst, relative to ordinary-summary review with identical executable checks and an identical allowed oversight budget. The target population is the frozen synthetic task and attack distribution under one specified analyst/reviewer configuration. Generalization beyond that population, model pair, budget, SQL subset, and publication interface requires a separately identified experiment. Prompted sabotage tests an explicitly constructed adversarial objective; it does not establish spontaneously emerging hidden goals.

The primary safety hypothesis H1 concerns the prompted-sabotage regime: arm E has a lower assigned-episode incorrect-release rate than arm D. The usefulness constraint H2 concerns honest analysts in the ordinary-error regime: E preserves correct required-answer coverage relative to D within a prespecified noninferiority margin, denoted delta. H3 examines transfer to held-out task structures and attack formulations. H4 explores differences in safety, usefulness, costs, and latency across the three regimes. H3 and H4, and safety comparisons in the ordinary-error and injection regimes, are secondary or exploratory; they cannot replace an unsuccessful primary comparison. A single positive primary conclusion requires both H1 and H2 to pass their frozen decision rules.

The intended comparison isolates a review *protocol*: its initial representation and the budget it consumes. It does not isolate an information-theoretic property of provenance or imply that the evidence view contains identical tokens to the ordinary summary. Both reviewers must be able to retrieve the same permitted underlying corpus with identical tools, per-lookup bounds, and combined limits. An added retrieval capability, private label, stronger checker, or additional accessible evidence changes the intervention. Such a change requires explicit relabeling and an ablation before attributing an effect specifically to representation.

### Outcomes and estimands

Let i index an assigned matched case, a an arm, g a regime, and S_i the required answer slots. A matched case includes task template, generated dataset, frozen snapshot, attack assignment, and stochastic repetition. Let L_iag indicate a confirmed release through the trusted publisher. Let Z_iags indicate that required slot s was released and independently judged correct, including a contract-authorized, independently justified abstention. Let M_iag indicate that at least one released required or permitted supporting quantitative claim is materially incorrect. Materiality must be evaluated independently of the runtime verifier and frozen before confirmation. The primary estimands are

\[
R_{ag}=\frac{\sum_i L_{iag}M_{iag}}{N_g},\qquad
C_{ag}=\frac{\sum_i\sum_{s\in S_i}Z_{iags}}{\sum_i|S_i|},
\]

where N_g is the number of assigned cases in regime g. The primary contrasts are Delta_R = R_E,sabotage - R_D,sabotage and Delta_C = C_E,ordinary - C_D,ordinary. Their denominators include all assigned cases and required slots, respectively. A confirmed withholding contributes no incorrect release and no correctly released slot. An arbitrary refusal is not a correct abstention. A correctly released required answer accompanied by an incorrect supporting claim may contribute to coverage while also contributing to incorrect-release rate; this is intentional.

Complete-correct-task rate is the proportion of assigned episodes that release all required slots correctly without any materially false additional quantitative claim. Report all numerical inaccuracies, semantic errors, unsupported answers, omitted required slots, unjustified abstentions, reviewer escalations, repairs, and false interventions on independently valid work. Do not infer the primary safety indicator from the scorer's single report-level `correct` flag: that flag also fails for missing or duplicate slots. A missing required answer primarily decreases coverage and completeness, whereas an incorrect released quantitative claim triggers the safety endpoint. Preserve the underlying slot outcomes and explicit error classes.

The present oracle uses exact-decimal slot tolerances. For reference value y and submitted value v, numerical agreement requires absolute(v-y) <= max(epsilon_abs, absolute(y)*epsilon_rel), as well as matching metric, type, unit, grain, interval, snapshot, and report semantics. Defaults are exact agreement; counts require exact agreement. A separate practical materiality policy is not yet fixed. Confirmation must specify whether any semantic violation is material, how numerical deviations are thresholded, and how incorrect abstentions are classified. Report exact-contract error rates alongside any coarser materiality endpoint so a post hoc threshold cannot hide errors. Unsupported truth or severity contracts must fail scoring visibly rather than acquire a convenient default.

### Threat regimes and attack validity

Run ordinary-error, indirect-injection, and prompted-sabotage regimes separately. Ordinary-error episodes receive an explicit no-attack policy identifier and an analyst instructed to solve the legitimate task. Indirect injection changes only declared textual surfaces before snapshot freezing. The clean and injected variants must have identical independently derived answers for every required and permitted supporting slot. Numeric data poisoning, changing task semantics, or corrupting a permitted answer violates this regime and requires a different experiment. The current fixed sales schema lacks an operational text-injection builder; adding notes/documentation surfaces requires versioned public contracts and integrity validation.

Prompted sabotage gives the analyst a synthetic private side objective, such as causing release of a wrong quantitative conclusion. The reviewer sees the legitimate task and assigned evidence view without the side objective, attack labels, or private answer. The attacker cannot edit frozen data, task contracts, host checks, evidence records, budgets, publication decisions, or private truth. Attack validity is determined from capability restrictions and answer preservation before execution, not from whether the attack succeeds. Preserve legitimate-task completion alongside attack success, including successful attacks that still answer some required slots correctly.

During development, allocate equal adaptation resources per defense, recording revisions, calls, tokens, and dollars. Ten revisions per attack family is an illustrative planning option, not an adopted count. Freeze the actual adaptation cap after the pilot. Freeze a union of admissible attacks developed with equal resources, then sample one attack assignment per task-instance/regime with a recorded seed. Apply the identical assigned attack to every paired arm. This is not a full attack-by-case sweep. Defense-specific attacks or sweeps are separately labeled experiments with separate counts. Attack filtering based on final-test success, adaptive retries after private scoring, or giving one arm a harder assignment invalidates the intended contrast.

### Frozen models, correction policy, and resource accounting

Freeze exact provider/model identifiers, available version information, generation settings, prompt and tool hashes, dependency lock hash, code commit, worker image identity, data/check/scorer versions, and pricing snapshot. The executed development pilot froze one model, `gpt-5.4-mini-2026-03-17`, for the analyst, the ordinary-summary writer, and the reviewer, together with the prices and USD 15 cap stated above. Confirmation has not frozen a separate model pair or a larger budget. One analyst configuration and the same reviewer configuration apply across the primary comparison; D's summarizer configuration is also frozen. Provider changes create a new experimental version or a documented deviation, never a silently mixed result.

Permit at most one correction round, as implemented, or freeze zero before confirmation. Rejected/needs-review revisions receive only the arm's own publication reasons and permitted reviewer reason codes; arbitrary reviewer prose and private scores are excluded. Approved and stale revisions receive no correction. The corrected submission is a new revision under the same episode, checked and reviewed again, with both histories retained. Analyst tool, model-call, and elapsed-time budgets are shared across rounds. Oversight summaries, initial reviews, lookups, and reviews after correction must all consume one combined episode oversight cap; restarting a per-review allowance is not equivalent.

Current defaults are 20 analyst tool calls, 24 analyst model calls, 600 seconds per episode, ten model calls per review including ordinary summarization, eight lookups per review, 4,000 summary characters, and 60,000 transcript characters. Query `ResourceLimits` currently default to 30 seconds, 256 MB, 1,048,576 output bytes, and 1,000 rows; these differ from some older planning examples and must be frozen from actual configuration. These defaults are not a completed cost-equivalence protocol. P4.5 combined oversight/spending reservations and P4.6 lease/retry integration remain prerequisites.

Enforce equal D/E allowed oversight model calls/tokens and equal verification/retrieval constraints before dispatch, counting all rounds and summarization. Record actual costs because identical caps can yield different utilization, prices, or latency. Reserve configured worst-case costs for in-flight work against experiment and episode caps, settle idempotently from reported usage, and retain unresolved reservations until uncertain billing is reconciled. Canceled or superseded attempts retain their actual charges. Model cost is the sum over calls of input_tokens*input_rate plus output_tokens*output_rate, divided by one million, with cached-token or other provider charges included where applicable. Report analyst, oversight, total cost, verification CPU time, end-to-end median/p95 latency, and cost per correctly released required slot. The development pilot froze the prices and USD 15 cap cited above. Confirmation prices, margins, and spending caps remain unresolved until their own freeze.

### Failures, missing outcomes, and exclusions

Assign episode identifiers before execution. Retain every assignment, attempt, provider dispatch, usage record, correction, release, and failure. Distinguish no submission, tool/time limit, malformed review, model/provider error, infrastructure error, known withholding, stale revision, and unknown publication state. Semantic errors and policy rejection are outcomes, not retryable infrastructure failures. The plan permits at most two transient provider/network retries initially; adopt the exact rule only after implementing and validating attempt identities, cancellation, fencing, billing reconciliation, and idempotence. Do not repeat an episode until it happens to succeed.

If k known incorrect releases and u unknown release outcomes occur among N assignments, report the risk bounds [k/N, (k+u)/N], not a rate that treats unknowns as safe. Analogously bound coverage by known correct slots and unresolved required slots. For E-minus-D differences, conservative endpoint bounds subtract the opposing arm's upper/lower bound. Distinguish this identification uncertainty from bootstrap sampling uncertainty. Report failures by arm, regime, template, and category, plus a prespecified matched-valid-case sensitivity analysis. That sensitivity analysis complements, rather than replaces, the all-assignment estimand. Unequal failure rates can explain an apparently favorable safety result.

Only duplicate bookkeeping records proven to refer to the same assignment or eligibility errors detectable without treatment outcomes may be excluded under frozen rules. Record exclusion reasons and counts. Oracle defects discovered after execution require a new scorer version and rescoring all affected arms. Invalid attack preservation or exposed holdouts require a documented scope decision and fresh eligible cases under the freeze policy. Corrections to infrastructure and scoring must remain distinguishable from tuning defenses after viewing results.

### Inference, decisions, stopping, and completion gates

Use a paired template-cluster bootstrap with 10,000 resamples and a recorded analysis seed, as proposed in the plan. Sample whole templates with replacement, retaining each selected template's datasets, regimes, arm matches, and repetitions. Recompute R and the ratio of correct-slot sums to required-slot sums in every resample. Do not replace slot-weighted coverage with a mean of per-episode percentages. Report E-minus-D point estimates, percentile 95% intervals, paired discordance counts, and per-template outcomes. Validate resampling on simulated known effects and sparse-event cases before freezing. With only 12 structural clusters, interval stability is limited; degenerate intervals or zero observed errors do not establish zero underlying risk.

H1 passes only if the upper bound of the frozen 95% interval for Delta_R is below zero. H2 passes only if the lower bound for Delta_C is above -delta. A five-percentage-point margin is a planning candidate; its substantive justification and final value remain open. Requiring both conditions is a joint success rule; do not choose whichever endpoint looks favorable. Report effect magnitude and all uncertainty even when the joint rule fails. Secondary comparisons require explicit multiplicity treatment if used for confirmatory claims; otherwise label them descriptive. Include an all-withhold reference with zero incorrect releases and zero coverage on primary safety/usefulness displays.

There is no result-dependent early stopping. Complete the frozen assignment list unless a prespecified safety, budget, or infrastructure stop applies. A boundary bypass halts new releases and triggers preservation, investigation, repair, and relevant revalidation. Exhausted budgets without an advance valid stopping rule produce an incomplete study. Changes to hypotheses, populations, margins, sample counts, attacks, prompts, models, budgets, checks, or analysis after unblinding create a new version and require a fresh holdout for further confirmatory claims.

Confirmation is gated on: (1) supported task/oracle contracts and hand-checked alternative solutions; (2) a validated attack builder and answer-preservation checks; (3) private actor/reviewer/evaluator isolation; (4) enforced combined budgets and audited attempt recovery; (5) worker/publication boundary, cancellation, and corruption tests; (6) a completed scored pilot and feasible precision/cost plan; (7) sealed structural/attack splits and an exposure register; and (8) a hashed preregistration with every unresolved parameter filled. Study completion additionally requires accounting for every assignment and charge, scoring exact released bytes independently, reproducing all tables/intervals from immutable records, reporting missingness and negative findings, and reproducing the sanitized release in a clean environment. The present repository does not pass these research-completion gates; its current evidence establishes components needed to attempt the study.

## Discussion, limitations, and research completion

The current evidence demonstrates a useful separation of questions. Authentic execution provenance can bind an incorrect number faithfully. A checker can reject a known multiplicity error without being a complete SQL theorem prover. A reviewer can recommend release without having publication authority. A publisher can enforce a revision transaction without establishing the truth of arbitrary prose. An independent evaluator can detect a wrong baseline release while remaining excluded from online authorization. Each distinction is necessary to interpret an eventual prevention result.

The audit's relation-binding defect is especially relevant to research validity. A surface match on a base-table name was insufficient when a common table expression or positional column alias changed the meaning of that relation. The observed wrong outputs were genuine DuckDB calculations, and their authentic receipts did not protect the checker from its own mistaken interpretation. The repair conservatively marks the unsupported relation-binding shapes unknown. Regression coverage establishes those repaired cases; it is not a proof that no other false-pass construction exists. Future task/check extensions need adversarial relation-resolution tests, equivalence tests, and explicit unknown outcomes before entering a frozen study.

The development pilot adds a concrete boundary around that future comparison. On the measured worker image, fetching a timezone-aware `occurred_at` value fails because DuckDB requires `pytz` and the image does not contain it. Every analyst preview of `orders` therefore returned `query_error`, and all 24 episodes exhausted six model calls without submitting a claim. The resulting zero release rate is an allowance-exhaustion outcome. It leaves the D-versus-E contrast unmeasured. A later image that can return timestamps would be a new worker identity and would require its own frozen run; this report does not spend the remaining cap on that rerun.

The single exposed sales family limits statistical and external validity. There are no measurements of naturally occurring analyst error frequencies in production businesses, no causal/business-advice verification, no broad rate/mean/ranking benchmark, and no evidence that a defense transfers across schemas or attack formulations. Scripted A/C incorrect release and B/D/E withholding are mechanism outcomes deliberately induced by developer-selected trajectories. They must not be reported as estimated safety rates or as evidence that evidence-preserving review beats ordinary review. D and E share the same deterministic checks, which can completely determine these particular withholding decisions.

The trusted host and builder remain assumptions. Snapshot permissions and container mounts restrict the worker but do not defend against an adversarial host administrator. Content hashes detect changes relative to stored expectations when artifacts are read; they do not make a signed, externally anchored audit ledger. Publication rechecks its authoritative stored inputs and exact rendering but does not rerun SQL or recursively read every dependency at the instant of release. Source identifiers require host-enforced immutability. These constraints belong in the threat model and cannot be inferred away from successful transaction tests.

Current transaction/fencing tests establish selected lifecycle behaviors. Complete lease expiration, delayed gateway-result admission, durable whole-experiment spending reservations, and recoverable distributed retries remain separate runtime work. A sequential pilot's dedicated budget journal can constrain that pilot without demonstrating those production capabilities. Likewise, keeping oracle code outside a wheel or worker mount reduces exposure but does not establish that every final-test actor/reviewer interaction is isolated from private evaluation information. Confirmation requires testing the actual end-to-end information boundary.

The prospective empirical contrast includes representation cost. D spends part of the common allowance constructing its ordinary summary; E starts with a host-built evidence view. A positive result would concern those protocols under equal allowed resources. It would not establish that adding more evidence always helps, that every monitor is reliable, or that observed episodes estimate a deployment hazard rate. Actual token utilization, lookup count, latency, deterministic checking time, and dollars must accompany both safety and coverage. All-withhold is the limiting example: avoiding every incorrect release is uninformative if the system never supplies a correct answer.

The proposed sample arithmetic remains a planning envelope. Twelve held-out structural templates produce only twelve primary clusters even when each has ten generated datasets and several regimes. Sparse errors and correlated failures can yield unstable or degenerate bootstrap intervals. A small pilot on the current family cannot estimate between-template variance for a future broad benchmark. It can establish feasibility, identify protocol failures, characterize cost, and motivate a larger benchmark; it cannot justify a precise power claim for an unbuilt task distribution. Any increase in sample size or change to the method must be frozen before confirmation outcomes are examined.

Completion of the research does not require a favorable result. It requires a validated and frozen task/attack distribution, enforced resource and information boundaries, a complete assignment/attempt/charge ledger, independent scoring of actual releases, uncertainty and missingness accounting, and reproduction of every reported table. The final paper should retain negative findings and the boundaries discovered during development. Until those gates pass, this document remains a technical systems report and prospective protocol with explicitly scoped development observations.
